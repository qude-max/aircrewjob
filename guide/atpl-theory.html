Surviving ATPL Theory: 13 Exams, One Pass

Study strategies, question banks, and how to schedule your sittings so you stay sane through the toughest stretch of training.

In this guide The mountain, measuredFull-time or distance learning?Question banks: tool, not crutchScheduling for sanity

The mountain, measured

Thirteen subjects, from Air Law to Flight Planning, examined by your national authority with a 75% pass mark each. All sittings must be completed within set windows, and passes feed your "frozen" ATPL. Total study load is commonly quoted at 650–800 hours. It's not intellectually deep — it's wide, and the volume is the difficulty.

Full-time or distance learning?

Full-time classroom: 6–8 months, total immersion, expensive in living costs but high pass rates and momentum. Distance learning: study alongside work with short mandatory brush-up phases, cheaper, slower, and brutally dependent on your discipline. The honest question is not which is better but which one you will actually finish. If you've never self-studied anything for 400 hours, be careful about assuming you'll start now.

Question banks: tool, not crutch

Everyone uses banks (BGS, Aviation Exam, ATPL Questions). Used well, they're retrieval practice — the most effective study technique known. Used badly, they teach you to recognise answer shapes without understanding, which collapses the moment the authority rewords a question, and airlines will probe bank-memorised knowledge at interview. Rule of thumb: learn the material first, then drill the bank until you're scoring 85%+ on fresh mock exams, and for every question you get wrong, write one sentence explaining why the right answer is right.

Scheduling for sanity

Batch subjects into three or four sittings grouped by synergy — e.g. the physics cluster (Principles of Flight, Performance), the systems cluster (AGK, Instruments), the planning cluster (Flight Planning, Mass & Balance, Performance). Take the heaviest cluster first while motivation is fresh. Book exam dates before you feel ready; deadlines study better than willpower. And protect first-series passes — airlines ask for the percentage, and 13 firsts at 88% is a CV line that quietly opens doors.
