Airline Interview & Sim Assessment Prep

Competency-based questions, group exercises, and the sim profiles used by major carriers — plus how assessors actually score you.

The anatomy of an airline selection day

Most carriers run a variation of the same gauntlet: online aptitude and personality testing, a group exercise, a competency-based interview, a technical quiz, and a simulator assessment. Each stage filters independently — acing the sim won't save a poor interview. The good news: the format is predictable, and preparation moves the needle enormously.

Competency questions: the STAR trap

"Tell me about a time you dealt with conflict in a team." Recruiters score these against frameworks — teamwork, leadership, situational awareness, decision-making, stress management. Use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) but don't recite it robotically: assessors want the Action to be specifically yours ("I did", not "we did") and the Result to include what you learned. Prepare six to eight real stories from flying, work, or sport, and map each to two or three competencies so you can flex them to any question.

The group exercise nobody prepares for

Four candidates, one planning problem — a diversion scenario, a logistics puzzle — and assessors watching how you behave, not whether you "win". The scoring rewards people who bring quiet structure: summarise, invite the silent person in, time-keep, concede gracefully when wrong. The fastest way to fail is dominating the room; the second fastest is saying nothing.

Sim assessment: what's actually scored

Typical profile: raw-data ILS, some general handling, an engine failure, often in an unfamiliar type. They are not testing type knowledge — they're testing trim discipline, scan, workload management and crew communication. Fly attitude + power = performance, verbalise your intentions ("my radial's drifting, correcting left"), and if you're flying with another candidate, be the best monitoring pilot they've ever seen. A sim prep session or two in the assessment type is some of the best money you'll spend.

Technical prep without drowning

Review: principles of flight at the "explain it to a smart teenager" level, your current aircraft's systems, mass & balance basics, meteorology (jet streams, CBs, icing), and the airline itself — fleet, bases, recent news. Depth matters less than the ability to reason aloud calmly when you don't know an answer. "I'm not certain, but here's how I'd think about it" scores better than a confident wrong answer every time.
